Reasoning at Every Scale: Molecules, Cells, and Patients

Efficient and Trustworthy Multimodal Vision for Healthcare

Medical images are increasingly analyzed together with molecular data and patient signals. RES-Med will ask how to build vision-centered multimodal models that remain accurate, computationally tractable, and reliable when modalities are missing, labels are scarce, hospitals differ, and hardware is limited.

Proposed for: CVPR 2027 workshops, June 20–21, 2027 Venue: Seattle, USA (with the main conference) Format: half day, in person, talks recorded
About the workshop Topics Organizers

Why this workshop

Segmentation masks are used as inputs to risk models, histology images are paired with gene expression profiles, atomic force microscopy (AFM) maps the mechanics of single cells, and medical images are interpreted alongside wearable signals and health records. In our analysis of recent CVPR, WACV, ICCV, and ECCV papers, only 18% of biomedical vision abstracts mention computational efficiency, and 21% mention generalization, calibration, or fairness.

RES-Med will bring together researchers from four communities: efficient medical vision, imaging–omics representation learning, cell microscopy and nanomechanics, and digital health. We will ask every contribution to report accuracy, computational requirements, and reliability together, which we call the joint reporting standard.

Why a vision workshop? Every contribution will use images, video, volumes, or slides as a primary input or output; other modalities add context to a visual task rather than replace it.

Questions we want to answer

  1. When a modality is missing at the point of care, how much does a multimodal vision model lose, and how should it fail?
  2. Do image-derived representations contain biological signal, and how should that signal be tested?
  3. What gain in accuracy justifies a tenfold increase in computation or memory in a clinic with modest hardware?
  4. Which reliability checks (external sites, calibration, subgroups) should be standard for multimodal medical vision?

Research pillars

The workshop has four scientific axes and a set of shared constraints.

Pillar 1

Efficient medical vision and segmentation

  • 2D/3D segmentation, detection, registration
  • Lightweight decoders, pruning, quantization
  • Edge and on-device deployment
Pillar 2

Imaging–omics and biological representation

  • Histology with spatial or bulk transcriptomics
  • Radiogenomics and molecular subtyping
  • Biologically structured representations
Pillar 3

Digital health and clinical translation

  • Imaging with health records and clinical text
  • Wearable and physiological signals
  • Outcomes, workflow, and external validation
Pillar 4

Cell microscopy and nanomechanics

  • Atomic force microscopy (AFM) of live cells and biomolecules
  • Nanomechanical maps linked to molecular state
  • Learning for automated microscopy and analysis

Cross-cutting: limited labels and paired data · missing modalities · cross-institution generalization and calibration · interpretability grounded in biology or anatomy · compute, memory, and latency · fairness, privacy, and clinically meaningful evaluation.

Topics of interest

  • Efficient 2D/3D medical image segmentation, detection, and registration
  • Imaging–omics integration and biologically structured representations
  • Cell and molecular microscopy, including atomic force microscopy (AFM) imaging, nanomechanical maps, and their links to omics
  • Imaging with health records, clinical text, wearables, and longitudinal data
  • Learning with missing modalities and limited paired or labeled data
  • Cross-institution generalization, calibration, uncertainty, and subgroup fairness
  • Interpretability grounded in anatomy, pathology, or molecular mechanism
  • Benchmarks and reporting practices that measure accuracy, computational requirements, and reliability together

Does my paper fit?

Strong submissions (1) put visual data at the center of the task and report (2) accuracy, (3) a resource profile (for example, parameters, FLOPs or latency, memory, and hardware), and (4) at least one reliability check, such as an external site, behavior with missing modalities, calibration, or subgroup results.

Work on non-image data alone (for example, omics or EHR models without a visual task) is better suited to other venues.

Scope

We invite vision-centered work on medical and biological images, video, volumes, or slides, alone or combined with molecular data, clinical records or text, or wearable signals. We especially welcome papers that report accuracy, computational requirements, and reliability together.

Community and ethics

  • Responsible data use: state data provenance, licenses, and approvals; use patient-level splits to prevent leakage.
  • Fairness and shift: report external-site and subgroup results where possible.
  • Clinical validity: explanations should be tested against known biology or anatomy; research models are not clinical devices.
  • Students: student-led papers and volunteers are especially welcome.
