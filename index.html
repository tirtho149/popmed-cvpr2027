Proposed CVPR 2027 workshop — acceptance pending

Pixels, Omics, and Patients

Efficient and Trustworthy Multimodal Vision for Healthcare

Medical vision models increasingly work alongside molecular data and patient signals. POPMed asks how to build vision-centred multimodal models that stay accurate, affordable, and reliable when modalities go missing, labels are scarce, hospitals differ, and hardware is limited.

Proposed for: CVPR 2027 workshops, June 20–21, 2027 Venue: Seattle, USA (with the main conference) Format: half day, in person, talks recorded
About the workshop Topics Organizers

Why this workshop

Segmentation masks feed risk models, histology is paired with gene expression, and imaging is read alongside wearable signals and health records. Yet most methods are still judged on accuracy alone, with every modality present, data from one site, and generous compute.

POPMed brings together three communities that seldom share a room: efficient medical vision, imaging–omics representation learning, and digital health. Its organizing principle is a joint evaluation contract: report accuracy, resource cost, and reliability together.

Why a vision workshop? Images, video, volumes, and slides are the primary input or output of every contribution; other modalities add context to a visual task rather than replace it.

Questions we want to answer

  1. When a modality is missing at the point of care, how much does a multimodal vision model lose, and how should it fail?
  2. Do image-derived representations carry real biological signal, and how would we know?
  3. What accuracy is worth a 10× increase in compute or memory in a clinic with modest hardware?
  4. Which reliability checks (external sites, calibration, subgroups) should be standard for multimodal medical vision?

Research pillars

Three scientific axes, joined by shared constraints.

Pillar 1

Efficient medical vision and segmentation

  • 2D/3D segmentation, detection, registration
  • Lightweight decoders, pruning, quantization
  • Edge and on-device deployment
Pillar 2

Imaging–omics and biological representation

  • Histology with spatial or bulk transcriptomics
  • Radiogenomics and molecular subtyping
  • Biologically structured representations
Pillar 3

Digital health and clinical translation

  • Imaging with health records and clinical text
  • Wearable and physiological signals
  • Outcomes, workflow, and external validation

Cross-cutting: limited labels and paired data · missing modalities · cross-institution generalization and calibration · interpretability grounded in biology or anatomy · compute, memory, and latency · fairness, privacy, and clinically meaningful evaluation.

Topics of interest

  • Efficient 2D/3D medical image segmentation, detection, and registration
  • Imaging–omics integration and biologically structured representations
  • Imaging with health records, clinical text, wearables, and longitudinal data
  • Learning with missing modalities and limited paired or labelled data
  • Cross-institution generalization, calibration, uncertainty, and subgroup fairness
  • Interpretability grounded in anatomy, pathology, or molecular mechanism
  • Benchmarks and reporting that measure accuracy, cost, and reliability together

Does my paper fit?

Strong submissions (1) put visual data at the centre of the task, and report (2) accuracy, (3) a resource profile (parameters, FLOPs or latency, memory, hardware), and (4) at least one reliability check: an external site, missing-modality behaviour, calibration, or subgroup results.

Work on non-image data alone (for example, omics or EHR models without a visual task) is better suited to other venues.

Community and ethics

  • Responsible data use: state data provenance, licences, and approvals; use patient-level splits to prevent leakage.
  • Fairness and shift: report external-site and subgroup results where possible.
  • Clinical validity: explanations should be tested against known biology or anatomy; research models are not clinical devices.
  • Students: student-led papers and volunteers are especially welcome.
